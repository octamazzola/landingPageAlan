# PRO-45 · Render and Architectural Linework Hybrid

VPS 1.0.0 · EN MASTER · Copy/paste the prompt body below.

```text
Transform the provided architectural view into a refined hybrid visualization combining realistic rendering with precise architectural linework. Treat the source as a locked geometric, material, camera, and compositional reference. The input is the single source of truth. If information is unclear, incomplete, obscured, or low-resolution, preserve the visible evidence conservatively instead of inventing hidden design. Preserve the exact architecture, proportions, openings, structural elements, furniture, landscape organization, camera position, perspective, framing, crop, and aspect ratio. Reduce unnecessary photorealistic noise so the geometry remains clear. Overlay clean architectural linework aligned precisely with the rendered geometry. Allow selected lines to remain slightly imperfect or hand-drawn, but preserve accurate perspective and alignment. Use minimal hatch patterns, diagrammatic accents, or tonal fills only where they improve spatial understanding. Maintain the project's original materials and colors, improving only their visual realism through controlled texture, natural roughness, subtle reflections, accurate shadows, and balanced lighting. Use varied line weights: stronger lines for principal silhouettes, cut edges, foreground elements, and structural emphasis; lighter lines for secondary details, distant context, joints, and material boundaries. Lock the source viewpoint, perspective or projection, apparent focal length, framing, crop, and aspect ratio. Every expressive layer must remain precisely aligned with the original geometry. Produce an elegant competition-board image that combines the depth of a render with the clarity of an architectural drawing. Deliver a competition-quality architectural image with a coherent artistic system, clear spatial hierarchy, deliberate negative space, controlled palette, and professional publication finish. The style must support the architecture, never overpower it. Do not alter the project, misalign linework, outline every texture, or create cartoon-like black contours. Avoid excessive hatching, visual clutter, strong filters, oversaturation, text, logos, and watermarks. Also avoid warped geometry, inconsistent element counts, duplicated objects, repeated textures, plastic materials, impossible reflections, conflicting light directions, excessive HDR, bloom, halos, oversaturation, artificial blur, text, logos, and watermarks.
```
