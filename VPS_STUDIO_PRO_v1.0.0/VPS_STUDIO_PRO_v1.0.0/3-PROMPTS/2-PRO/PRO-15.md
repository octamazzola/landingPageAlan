# PRO-15 · Top-Lit Interior

```text
Transform the provided 3D interior view into a highly realistic architectural photograph focused on natural top lighting. Treat the source as a locked architectural, material, lighting, and compositional reference. The input is the single source of truth. If information is unclear, incomplete, obscured, or low-resolution, preserve the visible evidence conservatively instead of inventing hidden design. Preserve the exact geometry, ceiling configuration, skylights, courtyards, roof openings, structural elements, walls, furniture, materials, camera position, perspective, framing, and aspect ratio. Preserve the original viewpoint, focal appearance, verticals, and wide architectural depth of field. Improve only its physical realism and response to descending natural light. Use daylight entering exclusively through the existing skylights, clerestories, roof monitors, courtyards, or overhead openings. Use either direct or diffuse top light according to the source geometry and exterior condition, without combining contradictory effects. Preserve every material's identity, color, placement, pattern, and scale. Lock the original viewpoint, camera height, orientation, tilt, field of view, apparent focal length, perspective convergence, framing, crop, and aspect ratio. Correct only distortions introduced by generation, never the source composition. Create a realistic vertical luminance gradient, natural wall bounce, precise contact shadows, controlled contrast, and softly illuminated deeper areas. Produce refined editorial architectural photography in which top light reveals volume, materiality, and spatial depth. Deliver high-end architectural photography with clean geometry, realistic scale, physically coherent light and materials, natural dynamic range, controlled highlights, precise contact shadows, and restrained editorial grading. The image must read as real photography, not generic CGI. Do not create, enlarge, relocate, or reshape any overhead opening. Do not add openings, luminaires, furniture, vegetation, people, dust, smoke, fog, or theatrical light beams. Avoid burned skylights, artificial volumetric rays, excessive contrast, warped geometry, text, logos, and watermarks. Also avoid warped geometry, inconsistent element counts, duplicated objects, repeated textures, plastic materials, impossible reflections, conflicting light directions, excessive HDR, bloom, halos, oversaturation, artificial blur, text, logos, and watermarks.
```
