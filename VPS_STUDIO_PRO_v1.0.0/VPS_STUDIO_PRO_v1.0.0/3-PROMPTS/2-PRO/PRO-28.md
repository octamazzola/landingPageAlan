# PRO-28 · Activated Urbanism

```text
Transform the provided urban-design view into a highly realistic professional urban visualization showing controlled everyday activity. Treat the source as a locked street-layout, public-space, architectural, landscape, mobility, and compositional reference. The input is the single source of truth. If information is unclear, incomplete, obscured, or low-resolution, preserve the visible evidence conservatively instead of inventing hidden design. Preserve the exact roadway geometry, lane widths, sidewalks, cycle paths, crossings, public spaces, building frontages, street furniture, vegetation, levels, transit infrastructure, camera position, perspective, framing, and aspect ratio. Keep all entrances, paths, crossings, facade elements, landscape features, and circulation systems fully visible. Add a restrained number of context-appropriate pedestrians, cyclists, and vehicles only as scale and use references. Distribute activity naturally and avoid staged poses, crowds, congestion, or advertising-like behavior. Use coherent lighting, natural shadows, realistic scale, and balanced urban depth. Maintain all materials and urban elements exactly as designed, improving only their physical realism. Lock the original viewpoint, camera height, orientation, tilt, field of view, apparent focal length, perspective convergence, framing, crop, and aspect ratio. Correct only distortions introduced by generation, never the source composition. Produce refined professional urban-design imagery with a believable, safe, inhabited atmosphere. Deliver high-end architectural photography with clean geometry, realistic scale, physically coherent light and materials, natural dynamic range, controlled highlights, precise contact shadows, and restrained editorial grading. The image must read as real photography, not generic CGI. Do not modify the urban proposal or add kiosks, signs, street furniture, commercial displays, trees, lanes, or infrastructure. Avoid oversized figures, repeated people, malformed vehicles, blocked circulation, artificial crowd density, excessive blur, text, logos, and watermarks. Also avoid warped geometry, inconsistent element counts, duplicated objects, repeated textures, plastic materials, impossible reflections, conflicting light directions, excessive HDR, bloom, halos, oversaturation, artificial blur, text, logos, and watermarks.
```
