# PRO-36 · Atmospheric Architectural Collage

VPS 1.0.0 · EN MASTER · Copy/paste the prompt body below.

```text
Transform the provided architectural view into a refined atmospheric architectural collage. Treat the source as a locked geometric, spatial, camera, and compositional reference. The input is the single source of truth. If information is unclear, incomplete, obscured, or low-resolution, preserve the visible evidence conservatively instead of inventing hidden design. Preserve the exact architecture, proportions, openings, structural elements, furniture, landscape layout, camera position, perspective, framing, and aspect ratio. Maintain clear tonal separation between architecture, foreground, middle ground, and background. Keep edges intentionally layered but precise, using soft masks, paper-like transitions, subtle shadows, and occasional visible cutout boundaries. Every added element must support scale, depth, and narrative without covering important architecture. Use a cohesive muted palette with natural neutrals, soft earth tones, dusty greens, pale sky colors, and one restrained accent color when compatible with the source. Combine clean architectural rendering with carefully layered photographic cutouts, scanned paper textures, subtle grain, desaturated vegetation, restrained human silhouettes, soft atmospheric gradients, and editorial graphic composition. Preserve the material identity of the project while simplifying excessive photorealistic detail into controlled collage textures. Lock the source viewpoint, perspective or projection, apparent focal length, framing, crop, and aspect ratio. Every expressive layer must remain precisely aligned with the original geometry. Create an elegant competition-board atmosphere with strong composition, visual calm, and architectural clarity. Deliver a competition-quality architectural image with a coherent artistic system, clear spatial hierarchy, deliberate negative space, controlled palette, and professional publication finish. The style must support the architecture, never overpower it. Do not redesign or alter the project. Do not distort the geometry, replace materials arbitrarily, overcrowd the image, introduce unrelated objects, or use cartoonish figures. Avoid excessive saturation, decorative clutter, rough masking, repeated cutouts, text, logos, and watermarks. Also avoid warped geometry, inconsistent element counts, duplicated objects, repeated textures, plastic materials, impossible reflections, conflicting light directions, excessive HDR, bloom, halos, oversaturation, artificial blur, text, logos, and watermarks.
```
