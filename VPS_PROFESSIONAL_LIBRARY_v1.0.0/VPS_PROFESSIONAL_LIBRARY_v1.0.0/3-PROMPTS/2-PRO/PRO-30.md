```text
Transform the provided eye-level architectural view into a highly realistic professional architectural photograph. Treat the source as a locked architectural, public-realm, material, camera, and compositional reference. The input is the single source of truth. If information is unclear, incomplete, obscured, or low-resolution, preserve the visible evidence conservatively instead of inventing hidden design. Preserve the exact architecture, entrances, openings, ground levels, paths, sidewalks, pavements, street furniture, vegetation, surrounding context, camera position, eye level, perspective, framing, and aspect ratio. Preserve the original field of view and natural pedestrian perspective. Maintain believable vertical convergence, human-scale depth, precise contact shadows, and coherent foreground-to-background focus. Use lighting and climate consistent with the source and produce refined architectural photography that feels immersive, natural, and physically plausible. Add no people unless the source already contains figures or scale placeholders. Maintain the identity, color, placement, pattern, and scale of all materials. Give particular attention to realistic surface detail, joints, wear, reflections, and texture scale near the viewer. Lock the original viewpoint, camera height, orientation, tilt, field of view, apparent focal length, perspective convergence, framing, crop, and aspect ratio. Correct only distortions introduced by generation, never the source composition. Deliver high-end architectural photography with clean geometry, realistic scale, physically coherent light and materials, natural dynamic range, controlled highlights, precise contact shadows, and restrained editorial grading. The image must read as real photography, not generic CGI. Do not move the camera, alter entrances, widen paths, enlarge spaces, modify buildings, or add landscape elements. Never block doors, paths, or important architectural details. Avoid heroic distortion, fisheye effects, excessive wide angle, artificial blur, text, logos, and watermarks. Also avoid warped geometry, inconsistent element counts, duplicated objects, repeated textures, plastic materials, impossible reflections, conflicting light directions, excessive HDR, bloom, halos, oversaturation, artificial blur, text, logos, and watermarks.
```
