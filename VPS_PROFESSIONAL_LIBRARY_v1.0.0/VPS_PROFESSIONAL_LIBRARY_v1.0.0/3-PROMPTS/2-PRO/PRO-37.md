# PRO-37 · Mixed-Media Architectural Collage

VPS 1.0.0 · EN MASTER · Copy/paste the prompt body below.

```text
Transform the provided architectural view into a sophisticated mixed-media architectural collage while preserving the project as a locked geometric and compositional reference. The input is the single source of truth. If information is unclear, incomplete, obscured, or low-resolution, preserve the visible evidence conservatively instead of inventing hidden design. Maintain the exact geometry, massing, proportions, openings, circulation, furniture, landscape structure, camera position, perspective, framing, and aspect ratio. Keep the architecture clearly dominant and fully legible. The techniques must feel intentionally composed rather than randomly layered. Use linework to reinforce edges, openings, structural rhythm, and spatial depth. Apply a limited editorial palette based on the original project, with restrained contrast and balanced negative space. Allow small analog imperfections such as uneven paper edges, light registration shifts, brush variation, and visible layering. Build the image through a controlled combination of architectural linework, photographic fragments, torn paper, printed textures, translucent washes, graphite marks, ink accents, scanned cardboard, and subtle hand-painted surfaces. Use photographic fragments selectively for material cues, vegetation, sky, people, or context, without converting the image into a conventional photomontage. Lock the source viewpoint, perspective or projection, apparent focal length, framing, crop, and aspect ratio. Every expressive layer must remain precisely aligned with the original geometry. Create a competition-quality visual that combines technical precision with artistic expression. Deliver a competition-quality architectural image with a coherent artistic system, clear spatial hierarchy, deliberate negative space, controlled palette, and professional publication finish. The style must support the architecture, never overpower it. Do not modify the design, obscure essential spaces, use excessive decorative textures, or introduce unrelated imagery. Avoid chaotic layering, childish drawing, strong digital effects, oversaturation, text, logos, and watermarks. Also avoid warped geometry, inconsistent element counts, duplicated objects, repeated textures, plastic materials, impossible reflections, conflicting light directions, excessive HDR, bloom, halos, oversaturation, artificial blur, text, logos, and watermarks.
```
